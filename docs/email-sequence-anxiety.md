## Email 3 — Teaching + value

- **Subject:** The anxiety mistake that makes everything worse
- **Send:** Day 3
- **Goal:** A real "aha" — reframe fighting → noticing

> [First Name],
>
> Okay, here's the mistake I promised to tell you about:
>
> **Trying to make the anxiety go away.**
>
> I know that sounds backwards. Obviously you WANT the anxiety to go away, right?
>
> But here's what happens when you fight it:
>
> Your body interprets that resistance as MORE danger. So your nervous system amps up even MORE. Which creates MORE anxiety. Which makes you fight harder. And round and round you go.
>
> It's exhausting.
>
> Here's what works instead: **gently noticing what's present.**
>
> Not trying to change it. Not trying to fix it. Just… noticing.
>
> "Oh, there's tightness in my chest."
> "There's my mind racing again."
> "There's that familiar feeling of dread."
>
> No judgment. No resistance. Just gentle awareness.
>
> And here's the weird part: when you stop fighting the anxiety… when you just notice it with curiosity instead of fear… it often softens on its own. Not always. Not immediately. But more often than you'd think.
>
> This is exactly what I walk you through in the guide. The "Noticing What's Present" section gives you a simple framework for doing this without getting overwhelmed. And the "Something to Return To" section gives you those personal anchors that help you feel steadier when everything feels chaotic.
>
> If you haven't worked through those sections yet, do it today. Even just five minutes. You deserve to feel calm again.
>
> — Ally
>

## Tone notes — choosing direction A vs B

The brief asked about two directions:

- **A: Softer, less sales-focused** (more nurturing, less urgency, gentler pushes)
- **B: Harder urgency and scarcity** (deadlines, "this is costing you" language, harder pushes)

**Recommended for this audience: A — with light, honest urgency only where it serves the reader.**

Reasoning:
- The person opting in is already feeling raw. Hard urgency reads as more pressure on a system that's already over-pressurised.
- Ally's brand on the rest of the site is "compassionate, body-aware, not clinical." Hard-sell tactics break that voice.
- The journal is a low-ticket follow-on. It doesn't need scarcity tricks to convert — it needs to feel like a natural next step.
- "Honest urgency" (e.g. "this discount expires {{DEADLINE}} — after that the journal goes back to full price") is fine because it's *true*. Manufactured scarcity ("only 3 left! 24h only!") is not on-brand.

What the current sequence does:
- Soft, nurturing tone throughout
- Reply-driven engagement in Emails 1 and 3 (builds list health + relationship)
- Journal mentioned softly in Email 5, then again in Email 7 alongside the consultation
- Consultation introduced as the "professional support" option in Email 6
- Email 7 wraps up honestly: "I won't keep pushing — here are your options if you're interested"

If conversion is poor after the first send, the lever to pull is **Email 5 and Email 7** (the two journal-pitch emails) — sharpen the deadline language there rather than across the whole sequence.
